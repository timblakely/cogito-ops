The Idle Bench

Where cogito's unused silicon goes to work once the coordinator lands: the 3090s trade context ceiling for parallel-agent seats, an Arc card everyone has been calling an iGPU becomes the always-on RAG floor, the desktop's 5070 Ti joins as a preemptible utility node, and the cloud tier is spent frugally on exactly two jobs.

This document assumes two things its companions establish and does not re-argue them: the coordinator pattern — a high-reasoning cloud model delegating short, self-contained subtasks to role-aliased local workers, from LiteLLM Routing Teardown §11 — and the preemption mechanics a game-streaming GPU node inherits, from The Contested GPU. What it adds is the hardware answer: which silicon hosts which role, and the small set of changes that gets there without buying anything.

01The inventory, corrected

The cluster owns far more accelerator than its LLM stack uses — and the most useful idle piece has been mislabeled in every conversation so far.

Five nodes today, one prospective, one deliberately excluded. Only iggy does any LLM serving at all; everything else in the accelerator column sits idle or runs a single opportunistic tenant:

NodeSiliconRAMServing todayIdle capacity
iggy 2× RTX 3090 (48GB) · 24 threads 128GB qwen-3-8-fp8, TP=2 — the cluster's only LLM tenant Concurrency, not VRAM — see §2
kristeva Arc A380, 6GB dedicated · 40 threads 128GB camofox only; the A380 serves nothing The whole card, plus ~all 40 threads
nuc-1/2/3 Meteor Lake iGPU + NPU · 18 threads each 96GB each Ceph OSDs; immich-ml holds one i915 unit Deliberately little — they are the storage floor
5070 Ti node RTX 5070 Ti (16GB) · today's desktop 64GB DDR5 Not in the cluster yet Everything — the node §3 plans
laptop RTX 3070 Mobile (8GB) — — Stays out: it becomes the daily driver and Moonlight client

Kristeva's "iGPU" is a discrete Arc A380

Node-feature-discovery labels kristeva intel.feature.node.kubernetes.io/gpu-type: A380 — a discrete Arc card with 6GB of its own GDDR6 and XMX matrix units, not an iGPU borrowing system RAM. Every earlier discussion binned it with the NUC iGPUs, and that misfiled it: 6GB of dedicated memory on an always-on node is exactly the shape an embedding-and-rerank floor wants, and it is the load-bearing discovery behind §4. Worth a one-minute confirmation against the PCI id before building on it, since the claim currently rests on one NFD label.

The target allocation, in one picture:

The through-line for everything below: the 3090s stay a single dense always-on worker but get re-tuned for seats; everything small, evictable, or rarely used moves onto silicon already owned and currently idle; and the only hardware acquisition in the whole plan costs zero dollars — it is the desktop changing rooms.

02Seats over ceiling

The routing teardown established that parallel agents come from continuous batching on one endpoint, not from a second model. What it left sized wrong is who the KV budget serves.

--max-num-seqs 2 is sized for two sequences at the full 262k ceiling — the teardown's own reading is that the KV pool left after ~27GB of FP8 weights holds about two sequences at the advertised length. That is the right trade for two long interactive chats. It is the wrong trade once a cloud coordinator is spraying subagents at the endpoint, because the coordinator design itself guarantees the local prompts are short: the long context lives in the cloud conversation, and workers receive self-contained subtasks. KV cost is linear in tokens held, so the same pool can be spent on seats instead of on a per-seat ceiling nobody local uses:

SettingTodayProposedWhy
--max-model-len 262144 131072 Still generous for any worker turn or interactive chat; halving the ceiling roughly doubles the count of full-length seats the pool holds
--max-num-seqs 2 6 sweep to 8 The literal parallel-agent count. vLLM only charges KV for tokens actually held, so short worker turns pack far denser than the worst case
maxInputTokens (LiteLLM) 262144 131072 Mirrored in the same commit — this is exactly the advertised-context drift the teardown's validator exists to catch
--enable-prefix-caching keep Earns more as seat count rises: parallel subagents share a system prompt and tool schemas, so the Nth prefill is largely a cache hit
--enable-chunked-prefill · --max-num-batched-tokens 8192 keep What stops one agent's 100k-token prefill from stalling five others' decode
--speculative-config (MTP) benchmark Speculation pays most at low batch; at 6 seats the verify work competes with real decode. Probably still net-positive, but that is a measurement, not an assumption

Measured, 2026-08-22

The retuned endpoint reports a KV pool of 277,007 tokens (2.11× full-ceiling concurrency; ~6% block-padding waste). Load-tested through the proxy with unique (cache-defeating) prompts: six agents at ~8k tokens each ran fully concurrent — all completed together in 53s with zero preemptions — while six at ~55k collapsed to 2 running with the rest queued, three hitting the worker's 300s timeout. Two conclusions: for the coordinator's designed workload (short self-contained briefs) the answer to "how many agents at once" is simply the max-num-seqs cap — 6 today, 8 after the sweep (KV would hold ~17 at 16k each); and above the ~46k-per-agent crossover the practical binder is prefill bandwidth (~1k tok/s aggregate on unique text), not KV — six 55k prefills are ~5 minutes of work no pool size fixes. Real agents share prefixes, so prefix caching softens this in practice.

Backpressure belongs at the proxy, not the backend

Give the worker virtual key an rpm / parallel-request limit a notch above the seat count. A coordinator that fans out twelve subagents then queues at LiteLLM with clean, visible errors and per-key accounting, instead of stacking silent timeouts inside vLLM's scheduler. The escalation path stays honest too: a worker that fails fast is what makes worker → worker-escalated feel responsive rather than like a hang.

Who loses the 262k ceiling

Anything driving the local model directly — Open WebUI, Hermes — with a genuine >131k local conversation. The bet this section makes is that no such consumer exists: long context is precisely what the coordinator holds in the cloud, where prompt caching makes it cheap to keep. If that bet is wrong for some workflow, the ceiling is one flag to revisit — but revisit it consciously, because every ceiling token is a seat token spent.

The device-plugin fix graduates to prerequisite

The teardown flagged iggy's time-slicing (replicas: 4, so two cards advertise eight units) as a correctness risk worth closing. Once a second GPU node exists it is no longer optional hygiene: The Contested GPU's central scheduling rule — advertise no more units than a game session consumes — is only expressible when advertised units equal physical devices. Set replicas: 1 on iggy so the TP=2 worker's gpu: 2 means "both physical cards" by construction, and plan the 5070 Ti node's slice count around its session arithmetic from day one.

03The desktop changes rooms

Promote the 5070 Ti machine into the cluster and daily-drive the 3070-mobile laptop. The Contested GPU already wrote the hard part of this node's design; what is new here is what should live on it — and it is not a second worker.

Why not a worker

The arithmetic first: the only Qwen build that fits 16GB is the W4A16 at ~13.5GB, leaving ~2.5GB for KV — a cramped seat with a fraction of the context the main endpoint offers. And the structural rule from the teardown is decisive regardless: nothing on the critical path belongs on a node that disappears when someone starts a game, and after §2 the critical path is the six-seat endpoint on iggy, which stays put. A preempted worker is a broken pipeline; a preempted utility is a retry.

The utility stack

Small, stateless, warm-restartable tenants — the shape that tolerates eviction gracefully. Everything below reloads from the hot tier in seconds to a minute:

Tenant~VRAMJobCost of eviction
compact VLM 7–8GB A 12B-class multimodal at 4-bit for the occasional screenshot — the vision lane §4 sizes A retry, or the CPU path on kristeva answers instead
muse-glimmer (re-quant) ≤12GB Kept only for what it writes — the capability question the teardown left open. The 17GB Q4_K_M does not fit; a smaller quant has to be sourced first A stalled creative session, annoying and harmless
whisper ~3GB STT bursts; nothing holds a connection open Transcription waits until the game ends
heavy reranker (optional) 2–4GB Only if benchmarks show the A380 floor too slow for bulk re-indexing — §7 measures this before anything is built Indexing throughput drops; the A380 floor keeps queries alive

Not all four need to run at once — 16GB comfortably holds the VLM plus Whisper plus a reranker, or Muse alone with real KV headroom. Which subset stays resident is a config toggle, not an architectural decision, precisely because nothing here is load-bearing. How they ship, though, is constrained by the preemption arithmetic:

The roster must evict as one pod

Kubernetes preemption removes the minimal set of lower-priority pods that lets the pending pod fit. Run the roster as three or four single-unit pods and a Wolf session claiming two units evicts only enough of them to free two — the survivors keep running beside the game, degrading it, and keep holding VRAM, which deadlocks the DCGM free-VRAM gate that is waiting to admit the session. The fix is structural: advertise exactly what a session consumes (two units), and run the whole roster as one pod that requests both — llama.cpp serving several small models from one process, or multiple containers in a single pod — so eviction is all-or-nothing and The Contested GPU's mechanism transfers verbatim. This constraint surfaced in review, not in any running cluster; it is the first thing to verify when the node joins.

The rules transfer verbatim

  • PriorityClass −100 on every inference tenant, nothing on the game. The one-negative-number mechanism from The Contested GPU §1, unchanged.
  • Advertise exactly the units a session consumes. A Wolf session claims the GPU twice — compositor and app — so the slice count must make that claim equal the node's whole budget, or preemption never fires and both workloads degrade.
  • Weights stay in the hot tier. A node preempted several times an evening must restart from local NVMe, not from a LAN copy.
  • Gate the session on free VRAM, not on the pod being gone. The DCGM-gated admission policy from The Contested GPU §4 — the driver releases framebuffer asynchronously after the pod dies.
  • Never a bare fallback target. Anything routed here needs a target behind it; the aliases in §6 encode that, and the validator asserts it.

The zero-dollar acquisition

Gaming moves to a Moonlight stream from this node — which is exactly the working configuration The Contested GPU §§2–3 reverse-engineered from biggs.dog, runtime-class fix and all — and the 3070-mobile laptop becomes the daily driver and streaming client. The 5070 Ti earns strictly more in the cluster than it does under a desk: it keeps its game duty via Wolf and picks up every utility model besides. Nothing is bought; a machine changes rooms.

04Kristeva, the always-on floor

The RAG path must not live on hardware games can evict — retrieval breaking whenever someone streams is silent and infuriating. There is a 6GB Arc card on the calmest node in the cluster doing nothing.

The A380 becomes the retrieval floor

An embedding model (bge-m3 / Qwen-embed class, ~1GB at int8) plus a cross-encoder reranker (~2GB) fit the A380's 6GB with room to spare, served via OpenVINO Model Server or a SYCL/Vulkan llama.cpp build. Publish both through LiteLLM — /v1/embeddings and /v1/rerank — under stable role aliases (embedder, reranker) beside the worker aliases, so every consumer binds to a name rather than a backend:

  • Open WebUI's RAG points at the proxy alias instead of its default in-pod sentence-transformers, which buys a real embedding model and central accounting in one move.
  • A future ToolHive-style gateway has an always-on embedder as a hard dependency — Foreman & ToolHive §9's toolhive-embed is exactly this component. On Jory's cluster it shares a box with inference and their failures correlate; here the floor sits on the node least likely to be touched by anything, which is the right decorrelation.
  • Bulk indexing almost certainly fits too — an A380 running int8 embeddings should sustain thousands of chunks a minute — but that is §7's benchmark, and the heavy-reranker slot on the 5070 Ti exists as the overflow valve if it does not.

The honest integration caveat

llmkube's Model CRD has a configurable resourceName, so scheduling against gpu.intel.com/i915 is expressible — but every serving image in the fleet today is a CUDA build. Getting llama.cpp-SYCL or OVMS under llmkube's management needs a look at ~/git/llm-operator before assuming it is free. The fallback costs one abstraction, not the plan: run the floor as a plain Deployment and point a LiteLLMModel's apiBase at its Service, which is all the litellm-operator ever needs.

Vision towers on the CPU — where "I rarely use them" belongs

Rare vision should not hold 3090 VRAM hostage, and the swap-pool Gemmas that used to carry it retire with the pool. llama.cpp splits multimodal models so the projector and vision encoder stay on CPU (--no-mmproj-offload); on 40 threads a small VLM runs entirely on kristeva's CPU with image encode measured in seconds — a perfectly acceptable price for an occasional screenshot. And this is no longer speculative: Jory's llama-nvidia lane already serves Qwen 3.8 exactly this way in production — 140k context with the projector encoding on CPU — and it carries his only image-capable lane (The Portable Coordinator §2). The tiered answer:

  • Occasional, casual: small VLM on kristeva's CPU, always available, costs no VRAM anywhere.
  • Faster, when the node is free: the compact VLM on the 5070 Ti, with the CPU path as its natural fallback when a game holds the card.
  • Genuinely hard vision: the cloud tier — Luna and GLM 5.3 read images anyway, and rare-by-declaration traffic is what the paid tier is for.

The MoE experiment: 128GB of RAM is a model slot

DeepSeek V4 Flash at UD-IQ3_S is ~110GB — it fits kristeva's RAM with the OS and camofox beside it, and flash-class MoEs activate a small expert subset per token, which is exactly the shape that runs tolerably on a fat CPU. Tokens per second will be gated almost entirely by memory bandwidth, and kristeva's 40-thread part has never been identified — a quad-or-more-channel platform changes the answer completely, which is why §7 measures before anyone commits. The weights are already in the NFS archive, so the experiment costs a copy, not a download; a slow cold load is irrelevant for a persistent lane.

The bar to clear: around 5 tok/s makes this a real overnight and batch lane — summarization queues, bulk review, second opinions — that costs the 3090s nothing. If it lands at 2 tok/s, the cloud's cheap tier wins and the lane is dropped without regret. An experiment, not a commitment.

05The NUCs stay boring

The trio is the storage floor. CPU pressure there becomes Ceph latency everywhere, so their job in this plan is to keep not doing LLM work.

Three reasons to resist the temptation of 54 threads and 288GB of combined RAM:

  • They are the Ceph core. Every OSD they run backs every PVC in the cluster, including the model hot tier. An embedding batch that steals their CPU shows up as I/O latency in workloads that have nothing to do with LLMs.
  • The iGPU units offer zero isolation. sharedDevNum: 99 means an i915 "unit" is a scheduling fiction ninety-nine deep — fine for the opportunistic tenant already there (immich-ml), wrong for anything latency-sensitive or throughput-promised.
  • The RAM has the wrong bandwidth profile for serving. E-core-heavy Meteor Lake on dual-channel memory is the opposite of what token generation wants; 96GB per node is capacity without the bandwidth to feed it.

The one placement that does belong here: the vector database (Qdrant or pgvector) behind the RAG floor. It is I/O-shaped rather than compute-shaped, wants to live near the storage it persists to, and its clients tolerate milliseconds happily. The Meteor Lake NPUs remain a curiosity, not capacity — nothing in this plan is worth the OpenVINO-NPU integration tax today.

06The cloud tier, frugally

Two jobs only: coordination, and catching what the local worker drops. The role aliases come from the routing teardown; this section seats providers in them.

Role aliasProviderPrice shapeWhy this seat

worker-escalated Luna@max on the subscription live (rescoped 2026-08-22 — GLM skipped: with coordinator-driven escalation there is no volume cloud lane for its coding-plan economics to serve) Flat-rate; credit-optimal Short, cold-prefix, entered on a local failure — the credit arbitrage makes max thinking on the light model the quality-per-credit optimum

The OAuth path is ToS-gray — design around its mortality

LiteLLM can front the Codex/Responses-style OAuth flow and spend subscription entitlements as API-shaped traffic. It is your own paid subscription driving your own interactive-and-agentic use, but it is not the product OpenAI sells, so expect token-refresh churn and accept a nonzero chance of the path being flagged or closed. The design rule that keeps this safe: nothing may work only if this path survives. The K3 understudy seat and the metered planner-gpt entries are the hedge, and they already exist.

Frugality is enforced structurally, not aspirationally

  • One virtual key per role, each with a budget. LiteLLM's per-key accounting answers "what did delegation cost this week" for free, and a budget cap turns a runaway loop into a clean 429 instead of a surprise invoice.
  • Fallbacks stay asymmetric, exactly as the teardown argues: worker → worker-escalated is load-bearing (short cold prefixes make it nearly free), and the coordinator carries no cross-provider fallback at all — its prefix is the whole conversation, and failing it over re-pays cache-creation rates on everything.
  • A PAYG floor under escalation is a candidate rung. Jory's ladder gained an energy-priced neuralwatt backstop (DeepSeek V4 Flash at $0.104/$0.207) precisely because his "flat-rate" plan turned out to be dollar-capped and repriced mid-month. The same shape here: a cheap metered rung behind the GLM 5.3 seat, so coding-plan cap exhaustion degrades in price instead of failing — and his principle transfers with it: order encodes preference, not cost, so the ladder need not be monotonic.
  • Tell the coordinator the ladder. Jory writes the lane economics into the coordinator's own prompt; cogito's version inverts it — worker and reviewer seats are free, so the prompt says fan out locally by default and enter the paid rungs on failure, with per-key budgets as the hard backstop. His council rule transfers unchanged: high-stakes review takes verdicts from different model families (local Qwen, GLM 5.3, K3) and never lets one model grade its own work. The harness side of both ideas is The Portable Coordinator §2 and §7.
  • The validator grows three assertions: the embedder and reranker aliases resolve to live backends; no alias whose backend sits on the preemptible node lacks a fallback behind it; and the coordinator alias still has no fallback entry — encoding the cache argument so a future edit cannot quietly undo it.

07Measure before moving

Every decision above is cheap to check, and all but one remain unchecked — that one was settled by decision instead. In dependency order:

Settled without the benchmark: FP8 across both cards

The single-card W4A16 alternative was worth an afternoon's A/B; on 2026-08-21 the decision landed on FP8 spanning both 3090s now, trading a possible single-card latency win for settled quality and context. The alternative stays recorded in the routing teardown §10 as the fallback if TP2 throughput ever becomes the constraint. If that A/B is ever run, one rule from Jory's strix experiment applies: match the KV cache dtype across both arms — his FP8-vs-Q4 run initially measured a 4-bit V cache rather than the weights (protocol adherence fell while single-shot reasoning held) and had to be redone at q8_0 on both sides.

  1. nvidia-smi -L and CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES inside the running qwen pod, then replicas: 1. Confirms the time-sliced allocation really spans two physical cards today, then makes it true by construction. Gates every scheduling rule the multi-node future depends on.
  2. Identify kristeva's CPU and measure its memory bandwidth. One lscpu and one STREAM-class run. Gates the MoE lane entirely and calibrates vision-encode latency expectations.
  3. A380 embedding throughput at int8. Chunks per minute under OVMS or llama.cpp-SYCL. Decides whether the retrieval floor also covers bulk indexing or whether the 5070 Ti reranker slot gets built.
  4. MTP on and off at max-num-seqs 6. Keeps speculative decoding only if it still earns its VRAM and verify-compute at real concurrency.
  5. A seat sweep, 2 → 8, under synthetic parallel agents. Measure inter-token latency and preemption counts at each step with realistic worker-length prompts. This sizes max-num-seqs empirically — the one number in §2 chosen by argument rather than by data.

Then the moves. The §2 retune goes first and is unblocked today — with the endpoint's shape settled by decision, only check 1 (truthful device-plugin counts) gates it. The rest follow the measurements that unlock them: stand up the A380 floor and repoint Open WebUI's RAG (§4), join the 5070 Ti node and port the preemption stack (§3), seat the cloud providers and their keys (§6). Each is independently shippable, none requires buying anything, and the bench stops being idle one section at a time.
